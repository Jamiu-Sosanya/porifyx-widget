# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The core promise is easy to picture: paste one script tag, train it on your own pages and PDFs, and visitors get answers.
- Strict mode, which offers a human instead of guessing, is good taste. It protects the business owner's reputation, which is the real fear with AI chat.
- Human handover with an AI summary covers the seam where most chat products break, the moment a bot hands off to a person.
- Saved versions and one-click rollback show care for the parts nobody sees.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** Ten channels, five AI providers, lead scoring, A/B tests, automations, a shared inbox and four plans for four kinds of buyer. A solo founder cannot make all of that excellent, and a small business owner cannot see the one reason to buy. The pitch needs a paragraph, so the product is not clear yet.
2. **The first five minutes are unknown.** No user has tried it. Nobody knows if a clinic owner can go from sign-up to a correct answer on their own site without help. With a 14-day trial and no card, a confusing first session means people leave quietly and you never learn why.
3. **Seams the customer will feel.** Who pays for AI usage is undecided. If the operator's keys run out or a provider fails during a busy hour, the customer's website looks broken. Ten channel integrations each add a place where messages can be dropped or arrive twice, and each one breaks when another company changes its API.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing this does better than anything else, and for which one buyer?
- Walk me through the first five minutes for a 10-person clinic. Where does it get stuck?
- What would you switch off at launch? Which channels and which plans?
- Who handles support when the bot gives a wrong answer on a customer's site, and how fast?
- What does one conversation cost you in AI fees, and what happens to margin on the 29 plan? (Unknown. /founder-cfo answers it.)
- What do rivals charge, and which seams do their customers complain about? (Unknown. /founder-competitors answers it.)
- Which of the four buyer types actually wants this, and at what price? (Unknown. /founder-consumer answers it.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one buyer and one job (for example, "answers clinic patients' questions and books the callback") and say it in one sentence.
2. Launch with the website widget plus at most one other channel. Hide the rest until customers ask.
3. Settle who pays for AI usage, and test the provider fallback before launch.
4. Watch five real target users set it up without help. Fix every point where they stall before opening the trial.
5. Run /founder-cfo on AI cost per conversation, so the cheapest plan is not losing money.
