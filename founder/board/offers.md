# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market passes on two of four tests.** Small businesses lose leads when nobody answers the website at night, and businesses with 5 to 50 staff can afford 29 to 199 a month. AI chat for business is a growing category.
- **Time to first result is short.** One script tag, training from pages the business already has, and a free trial with no card. That takes most of the "time" cost out of the value equation.
- **Strict mode raises believability.** Answering only from the knowledge base and handing off to a human, instead of guessing, speaks directly to the buyer's biggest fear.
- **There is already a lot to stack.** Lead scoring, human takeover, nine channels and proactive messages are real parts of an offer. Today they are a feature list, not an offer.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It becomes a commodity.** AI chat widgets are a crowded category, and the brief gives no reason a buyer would choose this one over any other. With no clear dream outcome, buyers compare plans on price and features, and a solo founder loses that fight. How crowded it is and what rivals charge is an open question for /founder-competitors.
2. **Buyers have no reason to believe it works.** No customers, no case studies, no numbers on leads captured. Likelihood is the weakest part of the value equation, and nothing on the page fixes it yet.
3. **The target is too wide to reach.** Shops, clinics, SaaS firms and agencies have different pains and buy in different places. With no acquisition channel chosen, "worldwide SMBs" is not a market you can find. Whether any one segment will actually pay is an open question for /founder-consumer.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one segment has the sharpest pain and money: clinics missing bookings, shops losing carts, or agencies reselling to clients?
- What result can you promise in 14 days, measured in leads or bookings, not features?
- What guarantee can you afford? That depends on AI cost per conversation and churn, both unknown, for /founder-cfo.
- Do customers bring their own AI keys? If not, does the 29 plan lose money on busy sites? (/founder-cfo)
- Could you charge double to one niche and still be the obvious choice?
- What currency are the prices in?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one segment and rewrite the offer around a single measurable outcome for it (for example, "after-hours enquiries turned into booked appointments").
2. Get proof before a wide launch: 3 to 5 pilot users with real numbers on leads or bookings.
3. Add a guarantee the numbers can carry, priced after /founder-cfo has worked out AI cost per conversation.
4. Turn the feature list into a stack: done-for-you setup and training, an onboarding call, and agency white-label as clearly named bonuses.
5. Run /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer before the prices are final.
