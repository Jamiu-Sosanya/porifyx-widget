# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The product is broad and already built. A solo founder can ship it at low cost.
- The pricing answers real complaints buyers have about rivals: bills they can't predict, AI sold as an add-on, and plans that upgrade themselves.
- It can run on ordinary shared hosting, so serving each customer should cost little.
- The founder already runs other PoriFyx products. That could become a small shared brand over time.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No moat, and anyone can copy it.** The code started as a script on the Codester marketplace, so other people can buy the same base. The AI underneath is rented. Nothing here is ten times better than Chatbase, Tidio or tawk.to. A copycat could match it in weeks, not months.
2. **No way to reach buyers.** How customers will find it is not decided yet. Every small business owner already gets pitched AI chat tools. A product with no route to its first 100 customers is not a business yet.
3. **No small market to own.** It is aimed at e-commerce shops, clinics, SaaS companies and agencies all at once, worldwide. That means a small share of a big, crowded market, which is the opposite of a monopoly. Fair pricing is easy to copy, and rivals can drop their credit systems whenever they like.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one narrow group of buyers can this win most of first? Why that group?
- What do you know about that group that Chatbase and Intercom do not?
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost to win?
- What is ten times better here, measured against the next best option?
- In month six, what stops another Codester buyer, or a bigger rival, from copying the pricing and the features?
- Is there anything that gets better as more customers join, such as shared data, an agency network or integrations?
- How much time does this get, when the founder also runs other products?
- Why now, when big platforms are already giving away AI chat inside their own products?
