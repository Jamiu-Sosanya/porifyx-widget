1 Logging In

Navigate to your platform's login page — your administrator will have given you the URL (usually https://yourdomain.com/login). Enter your email and password.

💡

If you have forgotten your password, click Forgot Password on the login page. A reset link will be sent to your email within a minute.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

If your administrator has enabled 2FA, after entering your password you will be asked for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app (Google Authenticator or Authy). Open the app, find the entry for this platform, and enter the current code. Codes change every 30 seconds.

To enable 2FA yourself, go to Settings → Security → Two-Factor Authentication and follow the on-screen instructions.

2 Your Dashboard

The dashboard is the first page you see after logging in. It gives you a real-time snapshot of your platform performance.

What Each Section Shows

📊 Today's Stats

Conversations started, messages exchanged, leads captured, and visitors who opened a widget — all for today so far.

🔥 Recent Hot Leads

The most recently created leads with a score of 80 or above. These need your attention first.

💬 Recent Conversations

The last few conversations across all your widgets. Click any row to open the full conversation.

📈 7-Day Trend

A line chart showing conversations, messages, and leads over the past week to help you spot patterns.

🔔 Notifications

The bell icon in the top bar shows recent alerts — new leads, handoff requests, and system notices. Click to mark as read.

🏷️ Plan Usage

How many widgets, messages, and team members you have used out of your plan's limits this month.

3 Creating Your First Widget

A widget is your AI chat agent. Each widget can be customised independently, connected to different channels, and embedded on different websites. You can have as many widgets as your plan allows.

  1. 1

    Go to Widgets

    Click Widgets in the left sidebar, then click the + New Widget button in the top right.

  2. 2

    Give It a Name

    The name is internal — your visitors will not see it. Use something descriptive like "Main Website" or "Support Bot — UK". This helps you identify widgets in your list.

  3. 3

    Choose an AI Provider

    Select which AI model should power this widget. If you are unsure, leave it on OpenAI GPT-4o — it is the most capable option.

  4. 4

    Set a Greeting Message

    This is the first message visitors see when they open the chat. Make it warm and specific: "Hi! I'm Alex, your [Company Name] AI assistant. How can I help you today?" is much better than a generic "Hello".

  5. 5

    Click Create Widget

    Your widget is created. You are taken directly to its Settings page where you can configure appearance, behaviour, and connect channels.

4 Widget Settings — Full Reference

Click a widget from your list, then click Edit (or the pencil icon) to open the settings editor. The editor has several tabs across the top.

Tab: General

  • Widget Name — internal label for your reference
  • AI Provider & Model — choose OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, or Groq and the specific model version
  • AI Personality / Tone — Friendly (casual and warm), Professional (formal business language), Sales (enthusiastic, conversion-focused), or Technical (precise and detailed)
  • Custom System Prompt — optional advanced instructions for the AI (e.g. "You are a support agent for Acme Corp. Never discuss competitors. Always ask for the customer's order number before troubleshooting.")
  • Greeting Message — what the AI says when a conversation starts
  • Conversation Starters — 3–5 quick question buttons shown to visitors so they do not have to think of what to ask first

Tab: Appearance

  • Theme — choose from Light, Dark, Minimal, Bold, Ocean, Nature
  • Primary Colour — your brand hex colour (e.g. #2f5ee8). Applied to the header, send button, and active elements
  • Font — select a typeface that matches your website
  • Launcher Position — Bottom Right (default), Bottom Left, Top Right, or Top Left
  • Launcher Style — circle, rounded square, or wide pill
  • AI Name & Avatar — the display name and avatar image shown in the chat header
  • Hide on Mobile — toggle to prevent the widget from appearing on phones if you prefer

Tab: Behaviour

  • Auto-Open — automatically pop open the widget after N seconds of page visit
  • Proactive Message — send a timed message to grab visitor attention before they open the chat manually
  • Offline Form — when your team is offline, show a form so visitors can leave their details and message
  • Collect Name / Email / Phone — choose which fields the AI asks for during lead capture
  • Sound — enable a notification sound for new messages
  • Allowed Domains — whitelist specific domain names so your widget only works on your own site
  • Rate Limit — maximum messages per minute a single visitor can send

Tab: Notifications

  • Email Notifications — enter one or more email addresses that receive alerts for new leads and handoff requests
  • Slack Webhook URL — paste a Slack incoming webhook URL to get real-time notifications in a Slack channel
  • Discord Webhook URL — same as Slack but for Discord
  • CSAT — enable end-of-conversation satisfaction ratings and configure the prompt message

Tab: Channels NEW

See Section 7 — Connecting Channels for the full walkthrough.

5 Embedding the Widget on Your Website

Once your widget is configured, you need to add one small piece of code to your website to make it appear.

  1. 1

    Find the Embed Code

    Open your widget's settings and click the Embed Code tab (or go to Widgets → click the </> icon next to your widget). You will see a script tag like this:

    <script src="https://yourdomain.com/widget.js?id=abc123def456" defer></script>
  2. 2

    Copy the Code

    Click the Copy button next to the code block. The entire script tag is now on your clipboard.

  3. 3

    Paste into Your Website

    Paste the script tag just before the closing </body> tag in your website's HTML. The method depends on your platform:

    • WordPress: Install the "Insert Headers and Footers" plugin → paste in Footer
    • Shopify: Online Store → Themes → Edit Code → theme.liquid → paste before </body>
    • Webflow: Project Settings → Custom Code → Footer Code → paste
    • Wix: Settings → Advanced → Custom Code → paste in Body (end)
    • Squarespace: Settings → Advanced → Code Injection → Footer → paste
    • Plain HTML: Open your HTML file in a text editor and paste before </body>
  4. 4

    Verify It Works

    Visit your website in a browser (not just a preview). You should see the chat launcher button (usually bottom right). Click it — the chat window should open with your greeting message.

    ⚠️

    If the widget does not appear, check that your domain is either empty or matches exactly what is in Allowed Domains in widget settings.

6 Training the Knowledge Base

The knowledge base is the library of information your AI uses to answer questions. An untrained AI gives generic answers; a trained AI gives answers specific to your business.

  1. 1

    Open the Knowledge Base

    Go to Widgets → your widget → Knowledge Base. Click + New Knowledge Base and give it a name (e.g. "Product FAQs 2026").

  2. 2

    Add Training Sources

    Click Add Source and choose the type of content:

    • PDF Upload — browse and upload a document file. Best for manuals, brochures, policies
    • Website URL — paste a web page URL. The system fetches and extracts all the text from that page
    • Sitemap — paste your sitemap.xml URL to crawl your entire website at once
    • Text — type or paste content directly — great for FAQs, product descriptions

    Add as many sources as you need. The more comprehensive your content, the better the AI performs.

  3. 3

    Click Train

    Once all sources are added, click Train Knowledge Base. The system processes each source in the background — breaking it into paragraphs and converting them into searchable embeddings. This typically takes 30 seconds to 5 minutes depending on the amount of content.

  4. 4

    Activate the Knowledge Base

    Once training is complete (green status), click Set as Active. Your widget will now use this knowledge base for all future conversations.

💡

Best practice: Start with your most important FAQ document and your main product/service pages. Test the widget by asking common customer questions. Add more sources over time to fill any gaps in the AI's knowledge.

When to Retrain

Retrain your knowledge base when:

  • You update your pricing or product details
  • You launch a new product or service
  • Your return/refund policy changes
  • You notice the AI giving outdated answers

You can run a new training at any time without affecting the current live knowledge base — it only goes live when you click Set as Active.

7 Connecting Channels NEW

Channels let your AI respond to customers on WhatsApp, Telegram, Facebook Messenger, Instagram, SMS, and more — using the same AI and the same knowledge base as your website widget.

ℹ️

The number of channels you can connect depends on your subscription plan. Check your plan limits under Settings → Billing.

Accessing Channel Settings

Go to Widgets → your widget → Edit → Channels tab. You will see:

  • A plan usage bar showing how many of your allowed channels you have connected
  • Cards for each connected channel showing status, message counts, and controls
  • An Add Channel button

Step-by-Step: Adding a Telegram Bot

Telegram is the easiest channel to start with because it requires only a free bot token.

  1. 1

    Create a Telegram Bot

    Open Telegram and search for @BotFather. Send the command /newbot. Follow the prompts to choose a name and username. BotFather gives you a token like 1234567890:ABCdef.... Copy it.

  2. 2

    Add Telegram Channel

    In your widget's Channels tab, click Add Channel → select Telegram. Paste the bot token in the field provided. Click Save Channel.

  3. 3

    Register the Webhook

    After saving, you will see a Webhook URL for your Telegram channel. The platform automatically registers this with Telegram's API when you save — no extra steps needed for Telegram specifically.

  4. 4

    Test the Connection

    Click the Test button (lightning icon). If you see "Connection successful", your bot is working. Open Telegram, find your bot by its username, and send it a message — the AI should respond!

Step-by-Step: Adding WhatsApp

⚠️

WhatsApp requires a verified Meta Business account and an approved WhatsApp Business API phone number. This process can take 1–3 days. Personal WhatsApp numbers cannot be used.

  1. 1

    Create a Meta App

    Go to developers.facebook.com → Create App → select Business → add the WhatsApp product. Follow the setup flow to link your WhatsApp Business number.

  2. 2

    Get Your Credentials

    From your Meta App Dashboard, copy: Phone Number ID, Access Token (a temporary or permanent token), and create a Verify Token (any random string you choose, e.g. "mysecret123").

  3. 3

    Add WhatsApp Channel

    In your widget Channels tab → Add Channel → WhatsApp. Fill in all three fields and save. The platform shows you a Webhook URL.

  4. 4

    Register Webhook in Meta

    In your Meta App → WhatsApp → Configuration → Webhook. Paste the Webhook URL and your Verify Token. Click Verify. Select messages as the subscribed field. Click Save.

Managing Your Channels

Each channel card shows:

  • Status toggle — enable/disable the channel instantly without deleting the configuration
  • Message stats — total sent, total received, and today's message count
  • Last activity — when the most recent message was sent or received
  • Copy Webhook URL — quickly copy the webhook URL for pasting into platform developer consoles
  • Test button — send a test ping to verify the connection is still working
  • Edit button — update the credentials (e.g. if you rotate a token)
  • Delete button — permanently remove the channel (requires confirmation)

8 Managing Conversations

Every message your AI exchanges with a visitor — across any channel — creates a conversation record. Go to Conversations in the sidebar to see your inbox.

The Conversations Inbox

The inbox shows all conversations with filters for:

  • Status: Open, AI Active, Human Active, Resolved, Spam
  • Widget: filter by a specific widget
  • Read / Unread
  • Starred

Click any conversation to open the full chat view.

Conversation Detail View

Inside a conversation you can:

  • Read the full message history with timestamps, who sent each message (visitor, AI, or agent), and AI confidence scores
  • Take over from AI — click Take Over and type a reply as a human agent. The visitor sees it as a response in the same chat window
  • Hand back to AI — once you have resolved the human part, click Switch to AI to let the AI continue
  • Apply labels — tag the conversation for reporting (e.g. "billing", "feature request", "complaint")
  • Star — mark important conversations
  • Resolve — close the conversation when it is complete. The visitor may be prompted for a CSAT rating
  • View the AI-generated summary — scroll to the bottom of the conversation to see bullet points of the key discussion and suggested next actions
  • View the lead record — a sidebar panel shows the lead extracted from this conversation (if any) with score, contact details, and notes

9 Managing Leads

Go to Leads in the sidebar. This shows every identified lead across all your widgets, sorted by most recent.

Leads Table

  • Filter by status, temperature (hot/warm/cold), score range, date, and widget
  • Sort by score, date, name, or company
  • Search by name, email, phone, or company

Lead Detail Page

Click a lead to open their profile. You will see:

  • Contact details extracted from the conversation
  • Lead score with a visual gauge
  • Temperature badge (Hot / Warm / Cold) and quality grade (A / B / C / D)
  • Status dropdown — update from New to Contacted, Qualified, Won, or Lost
  • The original conversation that generated this lead (click to view)
  • Notes — add internal notes visible only to your team
  • Timeline of all interactions with this lead

Exporting Leads

Click Export CSV at the top of the Leads page to download all leads (or filtered results) as a spreadsheet. This can be imported into any CRM — HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce, Zoho, or a simple Excel file.

11 Setting Up Automations

Go to Automations in the sidebar. Click + New Automation.

  1. 1

    Name the Automation

    Give it a clear name like "Notify Sales — Hot Lead" so future team members understand its purpose.

  2. 2

    Set the Trigger

    Choose when this automation fires. Example: Lead Captured with condition Lead Score ≥ 80.

  3. 3

    Choose an Action

    Select what happens. Example: Send Slack Notification. Paste your Slack webhook URL and write the message template using variables like {{lead.name}}, {{lead.email}}, {{lead.score}}.

  4. 4

    Save and Enable

    Click Save. Toggle the automation to Active. It will now fire every time the trigger condition is met.

💡

Useful automation ideas: Send a webhook to Zapier to add hot leads to HubSpot automatically. Send an email to your sales team with a link to the conversation. Post to a Discord channel when any new lead is created.

12 Running A/B Tests

Go to your widget's settings → click the A/B Test tab (or from Widgets list, click the beaker icon).

  1. 1

    Create a Test

    Click + New A/B Test. Give it a descriptive name like "Greeting Message Test Feb 2026".

  2. 2

    Configure Variant A

    Variant A is your control — the current version. Confirm its greeting message and tone.

  3. 3

    Configure Variant B

    Variant B is your challenger. Change the greeting message, tone, or other settings. Keep changes small — test one thing at a time for clear results.

  4. 4

    Set Traffic Split

    50/50 is the default and usually best. This means half your visitors see A and half see B.

  5. 5

    Start the Test

    Click Start Test. Leave it running for at least 1–2 weeks or until you have 200+ conversations per variant for statistical significance.

  6. 6

    Apply the Winner

    When the analytics show a clear winner (the platform shows a confidence score), click Apply Winner to make that variant permanent.

13 Account Settings

Profile Settings

Go to Settings → Profile. Update your name, email, password, and profile picture.

Team Members

Go to Settings → Team. Click Invite Member, enter their email address and role. They receive an email with a link to set their password. Roles:

  • Owner — full access including billing and team management
  • Manager — can manage widgets, conversations, and leads but not billing
  • Agent — can view and respond to conversations only

Notifications

Go to Settings → Notifications. Toggle which emails you want to receive: new lead alerts, weekly report summaries, system announcements, etc.

Billing

Go to Settings → Billing to see your current plan, usage statistics, and manage your subscription. Click Change Plan to upgrade or downgrade.

API Keys

Go to Settings → API Keys to create personal API keys for use with external integrations. Give each key a descriptive name and store it securely — it is shown only once.
